# SLU: the computer science lab

**Verdict:** the composition is Joe's choice, and the props, walk-behind desks and scene data are measured from it. Workshop acceptance of the assembled scene and of the walk-behind feel is still Joe's, in `art/review.html`. See [asset hashes and checks](2026-09-28-slu-lab.json).

## Composition

- The SLU scene is now the lab itself, not the McDonnell Douglas Hall lobby. Six Codex drafts tried two cameras: three true isometric cutaways (A) and three elevated front views (B). Joe chose **A2**: whiteboard on the left back wall, diploma and windows on the right one, blond exit doors at the far left, four desks in two pairs.
- A2 was revised with a fix list, with A2 as the final composition reference and A3 for the floor only. Four runs all kept A2's layout. **r3** was chosen: solid SLU-blue carpet, the basis-path-testing flow graph, and the lit monitor moved to the near-left desk so that the whiteboard, diploma and monitor spread across the room. The graph has seven nodes, an if/else joining at 5, and a loop from 6 back to 2, so V(G) = 3. Two runs drew double-headed arrows and were rejected; r3 drew an arrowhead at both ends of the loop, and the one at node 6 was erased by hand (a 19 × 17 px box on the board).
- The native master is Codex's own 16:9 canvas, 1672 × 941, unresized. `install-master.sh lab` upscaled it 4× (6688 × 3764). The reinstalled Upscayl 2.15.0 reproduced the previous SLU upscale byte for byte before the swap.

## Props, walk-behind desks and scene data

- The whiteboard, lit monitor (with its stand) and diploma are prop mattes. The four desks, each with its monitor, keyboard, mouse and chair, are scenery mattes. The front row is by the whiteboard; the back-left desk holds the lit monitor. All seven are polygons traced on the native master (`deriveFrom` plus `registration.mask`), each crop 1:1 with the 4× master. Composited over the plate, the mean difference per cut-out is 0.6 to 1.1/255, or 2.8/255 for the monitor, whose difference is only resampling outlines around the sharp green squares, with no offset.
- The front-left desk's chair base is partly hidden behind the lit monitor; its outline follows the monitor's top edge there.
- An early reading put the far-left monitor over the whiteboard tray and notched the whiteboard matte. The pixels show the monitor's top running from (510, 383) to (660, 347), below the tray, so the notch was removed and the tray edge is straight.
- **Walk-behind (Joe's call):** a cursor that steps onto a desk from between the rows is covered by it and cannot use its monitor; one that steps on from the chair side is drawn over it and can. The side is decided on stepping on, above or below a front line through the desk's front feet, and held until stepping off. Plain y-sorting would put every visitor behind a desk while pointing at its monitor. The engine rule is in buildout ticket 19; the workshop previews it with **Walk the cursor with the mouse**. The two front chairs, first cut as foreground scenery, were dropped: they belong to their desks.
- The lobby cut-outs (the Codex workstation and diploma, the chair matte) were removed.
- `src/lib/scenes/slu.ts`: prop rects are the mattes' trims (whiteboard 627,0 784 × 672; workstation 793,1021 284 × 239 since the matte was tightened, below; diploma 2374,167 333 × 457), reading whiteboard, workstation, diploma from left to right. The exit is the door leaves, 72,200 353 × 774. One depth region with the horizon at the back-corner floor line (537) and the foreground line at the bottom edge. No foreground scenery. `walkBehind` lists the four desks back to front with rect, outline, front line and props. `tests/sub-scene-geometry.test.ts` checks every sub-scene's rects, foreground and walk-behind data.
- `sceneLayouts.slu.arrival` is (604, 768), the camera Joe chose in the workshop. The first phone frame shows the right half of the doors, the whiteboard's graph and the left half of the lit monitor. (306, 1037), in front of the doors, showed mostly the doors and carpet.

## Open points

- In an isometric room the back desks are drawn as large as the front ones, but the depth factor still shrinks a cursor to 0.85 at the back corner, as on the overworld diorama.
- From the doors, the natural path to the lit monitor comes from between the rows, so a first visitor slides under it and must come round to the chair side to use it. That is the behaviour Joe asked for; keyboard and screen-reader use of the monitor's button is unaffected.

## Workstation matte tightened, 2026-09-28

On a checkerboard the workstation matte held more than the sliver noted at first: a strip of blue wall down the monitor's left side, desk under both ends of the bezel, the tan blend along its bottom edge and a corner of the dark chair behind it at the top right. None of it shows at rest, since the matte lands on identical pixels, but all of it would show under a highlight or a hover lift. The mask is now the monitor alone:
- the old polygon, less the wall (blue over red by 70 or more) and the desk and its blend (red over blue), cut at the monitor's right side and above its fitted top edge
- the holes filled, so the screen's light circle and bright squares stay
- traced with 1 native px of tolerance, so the edges are straight.

The native scale is taken per axis (1672/2845 across, 941/1600 down). The crop is still 1:1 with the 4× master (mean difference 0.00/255), and the prop rect is its new trim, 793,1021 284 × 239. The workstation still stands on the back-left desk, behind its front line. The hash in the JSON record is updated.

## After the code review, 2026-09-28

The whiteboard and the workstation are slanted, so their rects reach past their own pixels. The whiteboard's lower edge reaches behind the near-left desk's monitor. The workstation's top edge reaches behind that desk's back leg and chair base. A cursor hidden behind the desk could still have hovered either button there. Each now has a clip-path, its rect less the desk's outline, 1.5 px clear: 97 % of the whiteboard's rect and 96 % of the workstation's. A geometry test checks that no walk-behind outline covers any part of another prop's hit area or of the exit.
