# Overworld round twenty: the St. Louis Bread Co. east of Ted Drewes

**Verdict:** the small two-storey shop east of Ted Drewes is the St. Louis Bread Co. at 6607 Chippewa: a one-storey tan brick building with a wrap-round canopy, arched windows, a patio and a lettered sign, crisp at both tile densities, standing on the old shop's lot with one clear front door. Every automated check passes. One thing departs from the usual round and is Joe's to accept: the building is a raster decal over the upscale, not only pixels of the native master (below). Workshop acceptance in `art/review.html`, and the likeness to the photograph, are Joe's.

Joe (2026-10-01): replace the small cream-and-brick shop with the red-orange awning, at about world x 1260 to 1420, y 1640 to 1765, with the St. Louis Bread Co. down the street from the real Ted Drewes, drawn after his Street View screenshot; Ted Drewes stays exactly as it is; the building needs a clear front door, the door of the café's interior scene; the sign's lettering is a decal.

- **The round.** `sources/overworld-fix/rounds/round-20.json`, one 256 px tile (`breadco`, native 254, 372). The mask is a polygon round the old shop's lot, native x 358 to 448, y 469 to 527.5 (3633 px): the shop, its café tables, the bush west of it, the small tree east of it and that tree's lawn, stopping short of the sidewalk, of the small tree north-east of the shop (trunk at native 435, 495) and of the big foreground tree.
- **Six attempts, none kept as drawn.** Codex drew the tile six times from the fix list, with Joe's photograph as reference 3: a, b and c on a 288 × 192 tile, d, e and f on the 256 px square. Every attempt drew the building well (tan brick, flat roof with a pale coping, the dark seamed canopy wrapping the corner, arched windows, a glass double door, blank olive panels, two green umbrellas behind slatted screens, pink shrubs). None held the tile's registration: the stitch rejected a, b, c and e (drift 55.5, 51.5, 49.2, 18.9), and d and f, which it aligned (11.2, 7.6), drew the building larger than the lot, so the mask cut its patio and its roof. The model draws this building about 76 native px wide whatever the outline says.
- **Placed by hand.** Attempt a is kept for its drawing: the cleanest patio, the clearest door with its cream walk, the front panel widest. Its building is cut out along its outline (a traced polygon; the bush behind its patio keyed to paving) and pasted on the tile at 0.143 native px per attempt px, the size the lot allows: 67 native px wide with its patio against the old shop's 50, its back roof corner at native 385, 479.5. Under it the lot is cleared by construction: the old shop, tables and west bush to the paving's own colour (253, 240, 194), the strip behind the new, lower roof to lawn (158, 193, 54) up to the sidewalk, the small tree east of the shop to the lawn patch's own colour (176, 208, 58), the patch's west edge redrawn beside the new side wall. The recipe is `art/runs/overworld-bread-co/place-building.py` (local, ignored); its numbers are in the round spec's note. The stitch reports drift 0.0 and no pixel changed outside the mask; `tiles.py check` finds no dead-end road.
- **Why the building is a decal.** At 67 native px the arched windows are 3 px, the shrubs 3 px and the patio 12: out of the 4× upscale they came as blobs, the patio a brown smear (`art/runs/overworld-bread-co/` keeps the comparison). So the same cut-out, at the attempt's own resolution, is the decal `sources/overworld-fix/bread-co-building.png` (480 × 260, drawn 275 × 149 on the 4× master), composited over its native twin, which the native paste keeps 0.4 px inside the decal's outline so nothing shows round it. It is Codex's drawing, not a hand-drawn substitute; what is by hand is its place, its scale and the ground under it. This stretches the decal rule from logos, lettering and small props to a small building, and the plate there is model pixels laid over the upscale, not the upscale itself. Without it the choice is the blobs, or a bigger lot: dropping the entry from `decals.json` and reinstalling shows the upscale alone.
- **The sign and the raised parapet.** Joe supplied the Saint Louis Bread Co. logo later the same day (`sources/overworld-fix/bread-co-logo.webp`; Panera's trademark, shown as the client's own sign) and asked for the sign to look like it, then for the building's upper part to be resized so the sign sits in it, flush above the canopy with the roof's pale coping whole. `scripts/art/overworld/bread-co-sign.py` does both by construction on Codex's cut-out (`bread-co-building-drawn.png`): each column is split at the canopy's top edge and 34 of the drawing's px of the wall's own brick are let in (4.9 native, 16.6 world), the roof and coping riding up whole; the two blank olive panels are painted out in brick; and the logo's script, woman-and-bread mark and BREAD CO. oval, cropped from their panel, go on a sage board with a darker rim set into the front wall between the coping and the canopy, sheared to the wall's slope. The result is the one decal `bread-co-building.png`, 4.9 native px taller than its native twin underneath. The script's capitals are about 19 world px, 11 CSS px on a phone; the oval's BREAD CO. is too small to read there. The side wall carries no panel. The first sign, an olive strip lettered in Barlow Condensed with a plain wheat ear, is gone.
- **Install.** `install-master.sh twenty` upscaled the master 4×, composited the five decals, re-derived the plate, its tiles at both densities and the master-derived mattes, synced no geometry change (each re-derived `asset.json` differs only in its source hash) and listed nothing stale.
- **Read.** On the 4× master, on plate tile 2-3 at 1.25× and 2×, on the rebuilt composite, and in the 390 × 844 phone frame at 0.6 scale centred on the building: the building stands flat on the paving, its side wall on the lawn's edge, the patio on the paving west of it with a path's width left to Ted Drewes' lot; the perspective matches the neighbours (front wall to the viewer, side wall receding up to the right); the sign reads at both densities and in the composite, and is small but readable in the phone frame at 3× device pixels; the door is plain in the middle of the front wall. Nothing floats, nothing is cut, no fringe round the decal. The building has no cast shadow, as the shop before it had none.

## Round twenty-one: the ground round the building (Joe, later the same day)

Joe marked two spots on the installed building: on the west the paving was cut out beside the canopy and a white cut-out showed round the building, and on the front right a little path ran up to the bushes, to be filled with grass.

- **Where each came from.** The pale wedge west of the patio was round twenty's hand-cleared paving and the upscaled edge of the native building under the decal; the white line round the walls and the canopy's ends was the cream outline of Codex's cut-out, and the cream under the canopy's west end was the paving Codex drew behind the patio. Each row's lawn takes the tone of the lawn already in it. The stitch reports 0 px changed outside the masks and `tiles.py check` no dead-end road.
- **The outline, in the decal** (`scripts/art/overworld/bread-co-sign.py`): every cream px within three of the cut-out's edge, the roof's coping apart, takes the colour of the ground under it as round twenty-one laid it, lawn or paving, and the lawn runs on through the cream it touches behind the patio and under the canopy's end.
- **Left as it was:** the cream strip behind the building's east corner, up to the road's sidewalk, and the pale streak in the lawn west of the building, both the master's own.
- **Read.** On the plate at 2 px a world px: the lawn meets the patio and the west wall with no pale wedge or outline, its foot one straight edge; the lawn meets the corner bush and the east wall, the path gone. On the built site in Chrome at 1440 × 900 the door's link sits on the building.
