# Overworld: the rider pedals, its wheels turn, and it rides round the loop smoothly

**Verdict:** the rider is cut from a pedal sheet: seven pedal frames over two bold five-spoke wheels, drawn by the site one frame at a time in step with the distance ridden, on a smoothed path, leaning a little with it and turning round at the ends of the loop instead of flipping. `npm run art:validate`, `art:test`, `art:check` and `npm run ci` pass. Workshop acceptance in `art/review.html` is Joe's.

Joe (2026-09-30): the rider's wheels didn't seem to turn, its movement round the track looked stiff, and he wanted its legs to move: a better sprite sheet to animate. The old rig turned its wheels by the distance ridden, but their three thin cream spokes barely read at 20 to 40 CSS px; it followed the loop's 47-point polyline corner to corner and mirrored itself in one frame at each end.

- **The sheet.** `rider-sheet` is one Codex drawing against `rider-master`: a 3 × 3 grid, eight cells of the cyclist without wheels at eight crank angles and one lone wheel. It took six runs, each a draft for the next (`references/local/drafts/rider-sheet-t1.png` to `-t6.png`, with the guide drafts `rider-sheet-guide*.png`): the model draws the near foot in the front half of the stroke readily and kept redrawing the back half forward. The drafts aligned every cell on its hubs (t2), marked each pedal's place (t2, t3), then cleared the lower legs of the wrong cells and drew each pose as flat stick legs solved from the hip to its pedal (t4 to t6), with the prompt naming the near leg as the rider's left and saying where each foot is against the chainring and the rear wheel. The t6 sheet is kept. Its cells read, near crank from straight up, about 0°, 55°, 80°, 135°, 190°, 230° and 300°; cell 8, which earlier runs drew at about 345°, came back as a second 230° pose and is not used. Seven frames round the stroke, the widest step about 70°, read as pedalling at about eight frames a second.
- **Registration.** Every cell's frame lines up with the first's (the teal frame's best offset is 0, 0 in all eight), the hub circles 239 to 240 px apart; the chainring wanders up to 6 px against the hubs between cells. Seven cells are copied through a measured cell matte (`deriveFrom` with a full-cell mask, no redraw) into `rider-pedal-0` to `rider-pedal-6`, each anchored at its own rear hub (`rig.anchor`), so all seven sit within half a pixel of each other in the rig (x −12.5, y −298 to −298.5). The rest frame, near pedal down, is the rig's `body`; `pedal-1` to `pedal-6` follow in crank order (cells 5, 6, 7, 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Wheels.** The sheet's lone wheel has cream spokes, and on the site Joe could not see them turn: the rider is always over the cream path, which shows through the wheel, so only the dark tyre ring read, the same at every angle. `rider-wheel` is a Codex redraw of that wheel (the sheet's cell 9 attached, enlarged, as `references/local/drafts/rider-wheel-cream.png`) with its five spokes near-black (#1B1F1D): a dark teal tyre, the black spokes, a coral hub, kept lossless. It is scaled by its crop to 0.167, 159 × 160 px, so it is 0.673 of the hub spacing, as the master's wheels are, and cut twice, `rider-rear-wheel` and `rider-front-wheel` (the same pixels), each anchored so its centre lands on its hub within 0.2 px. The site draws both wheels first and the pedal frame over them, so the frame's hub circles and the rider's feet sit in front. The black spokes alone did not make them turn on the site, though (Joe, 2026-09-30): cropped round the hub on consecutive animation frames of the site's own scene canvas, they stood still. The wheel's angle is the distance ridden since the epoch over its radius, some 2 × 10^10 rad, and Chrome keeps the context's transform in double precision (`getTransform()` reads true) but rotates its drawing in single-precision degrees, whose steps at that size are 131,072 degrees: the drawn wheels jumped once every few minutes. The old rig had the same fault, which was Joe's first report. `turned` in `motion.ts` takes the angle within one turn, in double precision, before the canvas sees it (tested at server times up to 2.5 × 10^12 ms); read again the same way, the spokes step round every frame, anticlockwise heading west.
- **Keying.** Cut from the sheet as lossy WebP, every edge kept a one-pixel magenta ring (the 4:2:0 chroma bleeds the key). The sheet is kept lossless (`lossless` in the manifest), 1.1 MB, and its edges now keep only a faint lilac pixel here and there. It stays out of the site's build (`props.ts` excludes it; `rider-*` asset.json files were already excluded).
- **Pipeline.** `rig.anchor` (a part cut from a sheet is placed from its anchor, not the master's frame, and needs no master-sized source), `lossless` for a reference whose cells are keyed, and a draw order with the wheels before the body; the workshop draws only the parts in that order, so it shows the rest frame, not all seven stacked. `scripts/art/registration.test.ts` covers the anchor, the lossless round trip and the master-frame check.
- **Motion** (`rider` in `src/lib/engine/motion.ts`, still a pure function of server time). It follows the loop's centreline averaged 40 world px either way (triangular weights), which rounds the corners and keeps it within 4.5 px of the centreline; it turns round over 60 world px of travel (0.3 s) at the two points where it changes between riding east and west (world px 1088 and 3697 from the start line), its width easing through zero; it leans with the path's slope on screen up to 0.1 rad, fading as it turns; and its cranks turn once every 170 world px ridden, about 1.2 turns a second, a gear of about one and a half wheel turns. Under reduced motion it rests where it did, its near pedal down, facing east; its `sceneLayouts` rect moves 1 px up (y 2469), where the smoothed loop puts it.
- **Read.** On a checkerboard: all seven frames over the wheels, hubs on the wheel centres, no holes, feet over the ground line. On the site, headless Chrome at 3000 × 900 on the dev server: the lower straight heading east, the east turn (full width, edge on, mirrored), the upper straight heading west past the notice board and Joe's bike, the west turn, and the rest pose under reduced motion; the wheels' spokes turn from frame to frame (hub-centred crops, since the fix below). `tests/props.test.ts` holds it on the loop, no jump between frames, a lean within its clamp, a smooth turn round at exactly two places, a frame at a time round the sheet and a crank turn every stride.

For Joe to judge by eye: whether the seven-frame stroke reads as even (the steps are uneven, the shortest between the 55° and 80° frames); the far leg, which in the two back-half frames crosses in front of the frame tubes; and the black spokes turning over the path.
